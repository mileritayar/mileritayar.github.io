About

I'm a postdoctoral research associate in the Department of Psychology & Neuroscience at Duke University. My work asks a simple question with a lot of moving parts: how do people learn to control their attention?

I completed my Ph.D. and M.A. in Behavior, Brain, & Cognition at Washington University in St. Louis, mentored by Dr. Julie M. Bugg, where I mainly worked on how people learn cognitive control settings at different resolutions, whether and how they transfer learned control settings to novel environments, and how motivation shapes attentional settings. Before that, I earned my M.Sc. in Cognitive Psychology at Middle East Technical University in Ankara, Turkey.

Across this work, I use behavioral experiments and computational modeling to understand cognitive control as a learned, adaptive adjustment to the environment.

  • Cognitive control
  • Attention
  • Reward-based learning
  • Transfer of learning
  • Flexibility and stability
