Research

Learning where control belongs

My research program centers on cognitive control, the processes that let us focus on task-relevant information, resist distraction, and adapt to environmental demands. I'm particularly interested in how control is learned: people don't apply a single fixed level of focus to every situation, they build up item-, context-, and reward-linked expectations about where control is needed, and adjust their attention accordingly.

Two main threads run through this work. First, I study how learned control settings transfer — across items, tasks, and contexts — to ask how general versus how narrow these learned habits really are. Second, I use reward manipulations to trace how value learning reshapes attentional priorities.

Methodologically, I combine behavioral experiments with computational modeling.
