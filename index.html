About

I'm a postdoctoral research associate in the Department of Psychological & Neuroscience at Duke University. My work asks a simple question with a lot of moving parts: how do people figure out where a task needs their attention, and how do they hold on to — or let go of — that strategy once the task changes?

I completed my Ph.D. and M.A. in Behavior, Brain, & Cognition at Washington University in St. Louis, advised by Dr. Julie M. Bugg, where my dissertation examined how reward shapes attentional priorities in conflict tasks and whether those priorities transfer across contexts. Before that, I earned my M.Sc. in Cognitive Psychology at Middle East Technical University in Ankara, Turkey.

Across this work, I use behavioral experiments, computational modeling, and lifespan comparisons to understand cognitive control not as a single switch, but as a set of learned, item- and context-specific habits.
